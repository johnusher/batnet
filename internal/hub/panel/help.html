Start here

This box is a hub: a small computer (a Raspberry Pi) with a LoRa radio stick. LoRa radios reach a few kilometres with very little power and need no phone network, no internet and no electricity grid apart from the box's own power. The hub talks to the Meshtastic radios around it: other people's handheld radios and the repeaters on hills and towers that pass messages on.

This hub is this hub, on its preset. Its panel is at http://this-hub/hub/ on the home network, and always on its own screen.

When the power is out

The hub needs about 3 to 4 watts: a Pi 4 uses about 2.7 W doing nothing, the radio stick a little more when it sends. A small screen adds roughly 1 to 3 W (not measured yet). Messages themselves cost almost nothing.

To make it last longer

  • Choose a power mode (System, Power): High is everything at full speed, for mains power. Medium slows the processor to 1 GHz, saves power on the WiFi, and lets the screen sleep after 10 minutes without a key. Low slows the processor to 600 MHz, switches the Pi's own red and green lights off, makes the screen simpler (its lowest resolution and refresh rate, nothing moving) and lets it sleep after 2 minutes. A key or the mouse wakes it. In every mode the radio, the log, the sound and the LED keep working.
  • Unplug the screen when nobody looks at it: the hub stops drawing it at once, and starts again by itself when you plug it back in. The screen is the biggest saving.
  • Keep the hub listening rather than sending: Settings, Say hello: never.
  • Switch the WiFi mesh off (Settings) if there are no other batnet hubs nearby: its USB stick uses about 1 W.
  • Check the power: System, Power says whether the supply is good. Too weak means the Pi may stop: use a better power bank or a shorter, thicker cable.

Switching off and on

  • Best: System, Switch off (or sudo poweroff), wait until the green light stops blinking, then unplug.
  • If you have to, just pull the plug. The hub survives it; the log loses at most the last 30 seconds.
  • Plug in again: everything starts by itself in about 25 seconds. If logging was on, it carries on in a new file.

The clock after a power cut

The Pi has no clock battery. With no internet it starts from the time it saved a minute before the power went, so it is behind by however long it was off. The easy way: open the panel on a phone or laptop (on the hub's own WiFi, below), go to System, and press Set the hub's clock from this device; the button shows up when the two clocks differ by more than 2 minutes. Or by hand: sudo date -s "2026-10-21 19:30". The log also records the seconds since the hub started, so times can be corrected later.

When there is no network

In a power cut the home router is off too. What still works: the radio, messages by radio, the log, and the hub's own screen and keyboard. What doesn't: the panel from phones and laptops over WiFi, the map's streets, and setting the clock from the internet.

Ways to reach the hub

  1. Its own screen and keyboard. Needs nothing else. If the screen shows a login prompt instead of the panel, press Ctrl+Alt+F1.
  2. A network cable straight from a laptop into the hub's network socket. Then open http://this-hub.local/hub/. This should work without any router (the two find each other by themselves), but it has not been tried yet.
  3. The hub's own WiFi. The hub can be a WiFi network itself, for your phone and laptop. See below.

The hub's own WiFi

Normally the hub joins your home WiFi. When the router is off, it can make a WiFi network of its own:

  • By itself: when there has been no home WiFi for 3 minutes, the hub starts its own. (You can switch this off, or change the minutes, in System, WiFi.)
  • From its screen: System, Start the hub's own WiFi.
  • With the button (if one is wired, see "Sound and light"): hold it for 3 seconds.

The hub's screen then shows the network's name, its password and a QR code. Point a phone's camera at the code to join, or type the password. Then open http://10.42.0.1/hub/ for this panel, or http://10.42.0.1/ for the house app. There is no internet on this network.

Back to the home WiFi: the button is always there while the hub runs its own (System, Back to home WiFi). A restart always comes back to the home WiFi too. If the hub started its own WiFi by itself, it goes back by itself when the home WiFi returns and nobody is connected to the hub.

Neighbours. The hub's own WiFi has a password, so only the people you give it to can join. An admin can tick Let neighbours in (System, WiFi): then it has no password, anyone in range can join and use the house app (every new account still needs an admin's yes), and the panel asks everyone for the hub PIN. The hub shuts everything else (SSH, the radio's own controls) on that network. Untick it to close it again.

Other WiFi networks. System, WiFi, Look for networks lists those in range; Join… asks for the password. The hub remembers them; Make it the default is the one it joins first at boot, Forget removes one.

A terminal

On the hub's own keyboard: System, Open a terminal (or Ctrl+Alt+F2). Log in with the Pi's user name and password, and the commands below work there. Ctrl+Alt+F1 goes back to the panel. (For safety the terminal opens only on the hub's own screen, never in a browser.)

Sound, light and button

Sound. The hub plays a chime for a message for it, a message to everyone (at most once in 30 seconds), and an answer to one of its own messages. It plays on a USB speaker if one is plugged in, otherwise on the Pi's audio jack (the round socket next to the HDMI sockets): plug in a powered speaker or headphones. The Pi can't tell whether anything is plugged into the jack, so press Play a test sound (System, Sound). There you can also choose the volume, which chimes you want, quiet hours (only messages for the hub sound at night), and speech: the hub can say who a message is from, and read it out.

An LED. The hub blinks an LED three times for a message for it, and keeps blinking slowly until the message is read. Wiring: the LED's long leg through a 330 Ω resistor to GPIO 17 (pin 11), its short leg to a ground pin (pin 9). Pins are counted from the end near the SD card: pin 1 is the corner pin by the card, odd numbers on the inside row. Press Flash the LED to check.

A button. A push button between GPIO 27 (pin 13) and a ground pin (pin 14), no resistor needed. A short press marks the messages read (the LED stops); holding it for 3 seconds starts or stops the hub's own WiFi, with two long blinks. Both pins can be changed in System, Light and button.

Translate

The hub can translate messages between German and English by itself, without the internet. Under a message in German there is a Translate link; the English shows under the original, marked as a machine translation. To write in German: type in English, press To German next to Send, check the German, then send it. System, Translator can translate every message without asking.

It takes a second or two per message (a few seconds for the first one after a restart). Machine translation gets slang, abbreviations and typos wrong sometimes: the original always stays.

Something is wrong

"Radio offline"
Is the radio stick plugged in? (Settings, This Pi, "LoRa stick".) Unplug it, wait 5 seconds, plug it back; the radio restarts by itself. Still offline: sudo systemctl restart meshtasticd.
No other radios heard
Most often the antenna: indoors this hub heard nothing for an hour, near the roof 32 radios in 20 minutes. Put the antenna outside or high up, by a window at least. Then the preset: radios only hear others on the same one, and Berlin's mesh uses MediumFast (Settings, Radio). The Traffic view shows whether anything at all arrives, even unreadable.
Lots of "unreadable" in Traffic
With a weak signal (near the dashed line) the radio is too far or the antenna too low. With a strong signal, packets collided: the channel is busy, nothing to fix here.
A message says "Not delivered"
The other radio is off, out of reach, or doesn't know this hub's key yet (that comes when it hears this hub say hello). Try again later, or write to everyone.
The screen is black
It may be asleep (in Medium and Low power): press a key or move the mouse. Wait a minute after switching on. A screen plugged in later starts by itself within a few seconds. Still nothing: the screen belongs in the HDMI socket next to the power socket (HDMI 0); then sudo systemctl restart batnet-screen.
The phone can't find the hub's WiFi
Is it on? System, WiFi says so, with its name. Phones list a new network after a few seconds; stand near the hub. The hub can't be on the home WiFi and its own at once.
No sound
System, Sound, Play a test sound. Is the speaker switched on and plugged into the jack (or a USB speaker into a USB socket)? Is Sound on, the volume up, and is it quiet hours?
The panel doesn't open on a laptop
Is the laptop on the same WiFi? Try the address shown on the hub's screen under Settings instead of the name. Without a router, see "When there is no network".
Forgot the hub PIN
It is on the hub's own screen under Settings, The house app. Or: sudo cat /var/lib/batnet/hub-pin.
The time is wrong
See "The clock after a power cut".
The card is full
Settings, This Pi, "Card free". Old logs are in /var/lib/batnet/logs/; download them first (Settings, Log), then delete the oldest.
It is hot
Up to about 70 °C is fine; above 80 °C the Pi slows down to protect itself. Give it air and shade. Low power (System, Power) also keeps it cooler.
Everything is stuck
The hub restarts its own programs when they hang, and the Pi reboots itself if the whole system hangs. If nothing moves for a few minutes: pull the power, wait 5 seconds, plug it in again.

Radio words

LoRa
"long range": a way of sending small messages slowly, a few kilometres, with very little power.
Meshtastic
the free software most LoRa radios run; every radio passes others' messages on, so they hop further.
Preset
the speed setting. Radios only hear others on the same one. LongFast reaches furthest but is slow, so a busy city clogs; MediumFast is three times faster and what Berlin uses.
Relay, hop
a radio that passed a message on. "Heard directly" means no relay in between; most messages here come through a few relays.
SNR
how far a signal stands above the background noise, in dB. LoRa can read signals below the noise: MediumFast down to about −12.5 dB, LongFast to about −17.5 dB. Higher is better.
RSSI
how strong the signal is, in dBm; −120 is very faint. SNR says more about whether it can be read.
Unreadable
heard but damaged: too weak, or two radios sent at once.
Encrypted
sent on someone else's private channel. Anyone can see that it was sent, only its group can read it.
Public channel
Meshtastic's open channel: everyone with a radio nearby can read it.
Airtime, duty cycle
how long a radio is on the air. In this band a radio may send at most 10% of each hour, and the software keeps to it.
Node ID
a radio's number, like !a1b2c3d4. The short name (up to four letters, or one emoji) is what small screens show.
Access point, hotspot
a WiFi network that phones and laptops join. The hub's own WiFi is one.
Antenna gain, dBi
how much an antenna focuses the signal; a 5 dBi antenna hears and reaches further than a stubby one.

Good manners on the air

  • Never switch the radio on without its antenna screwed on. Sending into no antenna can damage it.
  • The public channel is public: don't send anything private there. A message to one radio is private once the two radios know each other's keys, which they learn when they hear each other say hello.
  • Say hello rarely. Every hello asks every radio in range to answer, and in a busy mesh that crowds everyone out.
  • Stay on the preset your neighbours use. A new preset splits the mesh and leaves people out.
  • Positions are shared on purpose by the people who choose to. This hub does not send its own.
